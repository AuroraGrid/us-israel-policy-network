# Methodology

The U.S.–Israel Policy Network uses AURORA GRID and RECORD LOCK methods to evaluate institutional relationships, documented influence, policy mechanisms, and contested claims.

## Evidence classes

- **Verified fact:** supported by primary records or multiple independent high-quality sources.
- **Supported reporting:** responsibly reported but not independently established by the project.
- **Analytical inference:** a reasoned conclusion derived from documented facts and explicit assumptions.
- **Unresolved claim:** plausible or consequential but insufficiently established.
- **Not established:** the available record does not support the proposition.

## Analytical sequence

1. Define the claim precisely.
2. Identify the named actor, institution, mechanism, and time period.
3. Locate primary records before relying on secondary repetition.
4. Separate association, access, financing, advocacy, and policy outcomes.
5. Test the strongest alternative explanation.
6. Identify the binding constraint and missing evidence.
7. State the condition that would change the assessment.
8. Preserve source dates and revision history.

## Guardrails

- No collective responsibility is assigned to a religion, ethnicity, nationality, or diaspora community.
- Evidence of influence is not automatically treated as evidence of control.
- Lobbying, campaign finance, diplomacy, military cooperation, intelligence activity, and policy outcomes are treated as distinct mechanisms.
- Association is not treated as culpability.
- Contested claims retain uncertainty labels.
- Anonymous or circularly sourced claims are not promoted into verified conclusions.

## Revision standard

A claim may be upgraded, downgraded, corrected, or removed when new primary records, reliable reporting, legal findings, or material contradictions become available.
